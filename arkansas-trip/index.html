Steve's Mecca

The Walmart Museum. Top of Steve's list, and — inconveniently for anyone hoping to mock it — genuinely the right call.

Three men fly to Arkansas and one of them wants to see where Sam Walton sold his first pair of socks. It is exactly the sort of thing a weekend like this exists to indulge, and it would be an easy target if the museum hadn't just spent two years and a great deal of money making itself worth the trip.

It closed in 2022, moved to a temporary space, and reopened on the Square in early 2025 at more than double its old size. The building itself is the point: the Terry Block building of 1888, housing the five-and-dime Walton opened in 1950. The rebuild added a second floor, a third-floor space overlooking the Square, interactive exhibits, and a hologram of Walton that answers questions put to it.

The construction was managed almost entirely behind a replica facade — cranes assembled and taken down overnight — so that daily life on the Square never noticed. Which is, whatever you think of the company, a fairly Walmart way to run a building site.

Free, self-guided, and open until ten on a Saturday night. No ticket, no booking, no queue to plan around. The only constraint is Sunday's noon opening, and that happens to be the one that suits.

The exhibit gallery

The main event, and the bit that doubled in size. Interactive displays and the Walton hologram.

Walton's 5&10

The original store, downstairs, restored. This is the actual room the whole thing started in.

Steve

The car

Steve is picking this up, as far as anyone knows — worth confirming out loud, because it's the one thing nothing else routes around. The airport is a half-hour out, the Momentary is a drive and so is the escape room. Nothing on the list solves it, and late September is not the month to leave it to the counter. Assume no bike rack: rental fleets almost never have them, which is what makes delivered bikes the better answer below.

Do first

A plan B for Salt Lake

Forty-two minutes is a legal connection and nothing more, and it now has a dinner riding on it. If the San Francisco leg pushes back at all you get rebooked onto a later Salt Lake–XNA flight and the eight o'clock at Bar Cleeta goes with it. Worth knowing what else Delta runs into XNA that afternoon — and worth having Paul's number to hand so someone can move the table rather than lose it.

Optional

The escape room, if you still want it

Sunday no longer needs rescuing — the Walmart Museum is free, opens at noon and needs no booking. The escape room is now a want rather than a fix, and it runs on fixed hourly slots that sell out at weekends, so it only works if someone books it in advance.

Check

Walking into the permanent collection is free, and the Bachman-Wilson House is on the grounds — but whatever headline exhibition is running in September will be ticketed and can sell out.

This page can't take uploads

A published page like this one is served as a fixed file. It has no storage behind it, so there's nowhere for a photo to go and no way for something Steve uploads to reach Paul. Anything that looked like an upload button would quietly save into whichever phone pressed it and vanish on the next refresh.
